### S01 label
Overview
### S01 title
Overview
### S01 body
Five apps that share one order.
Problem: Customers couldn’t see what was available, vendors missed orders, riders worked from phone calls.
Solution: One order model and status language across customer, vendor, rider, web and admin.
What the design solved: customers stopped ordering dishes that were already sold out, vendors stopped losing new orders in a rush, and riders stopped calling customers for directions. Every surface now speaks the same seven order statuses.
I led product design from first sketches to launch, working with two engineers per platform, the founders and the operations team. I owned research, flows, UI, the component library and design QA.

### S02 label
Context
### S02 title
Context
### S02 body
Food delivery in Enugu runs on trust and phone calls.
Most vendors took orders over WhatsApp and calls. Payment was a mix of transfer and cash on delivery, and riders were independent, often switching between platforms during the day. Dado wanted to bring the local favourites online without losing what made them work: personal, fast and flexible.

### S03 label
Problem
### S03 title
The problem
### S03 body
Everyone was guessing.
Customers ordered meals that were already sold out and waited without knowing where their food was.

Vendors lost orders in busy hours because new orders looked the same as accepted ones.

Riders called customers for directions and had no clear view of their earnings or hours.

Operations couldn’t see where an order got stuck, so support was slow and reactive.

### S05 label
Process
### S05 title
Process
### S05 body
From a status map to five products.
I interviewed customers, vendors and riders, rode along on delivery runs and spent a lunch rush in a partner kitchen ([twelve] conversations, [three] rides, one kitchen shift; sample numbers). Three findings shaped the product: availability changes by the hour, not the day; riders need fewer taps, not more features; and customers forgive a delay if they can see it.

### S06 label
Ordering
### S06 title
Customers only see what can be cooked now
### S06 body
Availability lives on the menu, not in settings. Vendors toggle “In stock” on each dish, and customers only see what can be cooked now.
A “New” order can’t hide. New orders sit in a dark card at the top of the vendor’s list with the total and a single “Process order” action.
